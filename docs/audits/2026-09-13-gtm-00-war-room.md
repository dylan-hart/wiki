**Date:** 2026-09-13 · **Branch:** `scarlett` @ `df032646a` · **Audience:** Dylan (maintainer), nobody else yet

## Premise: why Cardinal exists

Cardinal is built first for Dylan and his coworkers; public release is a bonus. Adoption would be nice,
not necessary. So the strategy below competes hard on product and conduct, but it is sized to a side
project:

- **Success floor:** Cardinal serves its original users well. Everything past that is upside.
- **Expectations:** the targets in docs 05 and 08 are "what good looks like if it catches on," not
  obligations. If the outside world shrugs, nothing is lost.
- **What doesn't relax:** the trust work (security, legal hygiene, honest disclosure) and the clean-conduct
  rules. They protect the people already using it and your name, whether ten people or ten thousand
  find it.
- **What does relax:** launch timing, growth tactics, services and revenue. Pursue any of them only
  while they stay fun or pay for themselves.
- **One new caution:** because coworkers use it, the employment-agreement check (doc 04 A1) matters
  more, not less. Work done "for the employer's benefit" is exactly what most IP-assignment clauses
  reach. Settle in writing who owns Cardinal before anyone is using it at work.

**Upstream Wiki.js is not stalled. It is shipping public betas, near-daily, on the same stack as you.**

- NGPixel announced a "stable alpha" on 2026-08-12, published `3.0.0-alpha.530` (08-30), then five
  GitHub betas `537 → 561` (09-02 → 09-12); GHCR was at `beta.566` today.

  group mapping, audit log, Prometheus metrics, sitemap, prerendering, analytics and comments modules.

  Node 26, REST. Their `CLAUDE.md` is the ancestor of yours.
- Upstream already has, in 3.x beta: multi-site, approvals, live collab, webhooks, passkeys, audit

## 2. The grand strategy, in one page

**War aim.** Become the wiki that a Wiki.js 2.x admin, a Docmost evaluator who needs SSO/2FA without a
seat fee, and an on-prem Confluence refugee all land on — and stay on — because it is the most
governable, most complete *free* self-hosted wiki, it brings their content with it, and there is a
real person behind it they can pay for help.

**"No prisoners" — the correct reading.** Be ruthless about *users, product, pace and migration*, and
spotless about *conduct*. You win Wiki.js users only with their goodwill, and that community likes
NGPixel even while frustrated with them. The one unrecoverable mistake is making NGPixel the victim in
public. Take the users, never the man. (Doc 02 spells out exactly where the line is.)

**The code is not the moat.** Both projects are AGPL-3.0. Upstream can legally merge your MCP server,
semantic search or importer tomorrow; you can port their betas' fixes. Durable advantages are the
things AGPL can't copy:

1. **Being where 2.x users land first** — the only working 2.x importer, then the best one.
2. **Responsiveness** — upstream's issue tracker is closed to the public, 138 PRs are open, ~33% of
   2.x help threads are answered. A public tracker with a published response time is a weapon.
3. **A throat to choke** — Requarks sells no support or hosting. You can.
4. **Governance depth** — classification, approvals, delegated site admin, page rules. Expensive to
   replicate, and not what an opinionated solo upstream has prioritised.
5. **Brand, trust and distribution** — a clean name, security posture done in public, packages in every
   app store.

**Beachheads, in order** (doc 03): (1) stranded Wiki.js 2.x admins — Postgres first, then
MySQL/SQLite/MSSQL once the bundle connector is finished; (2) Docmost-considerers who need
SSO/2FA/page permissions without paying per seat; (3) multi-site operators (MSPs, universities,
agencies); (4) Confluence Data Center on-prem refugees — requires a Confluence importer by mid-2027;
(5) EU public sector — only through partners, 2027+.

**Timeline — dated, not gated (revised 2026-09-13).** The original framing below was "launch when the
gates go green, worst case January." Dylan's call, given the confirmed pace (§1): **plan on the
assumption the current pace holds, because there is no guaranteed second window.** Upstream is
cutting a real tagged prerelease every 1–4 days and is 10/13 through its own "3.0" milestone. If it
reaches GA — or just a widely-noticed feature-complete beta — before Cardinal launches, the sharpest
wedge (the only working 2.x migration path, "production-ready while they're still beta") narrows or
closes, and arriving second into a market that already has a 3.0 is a materially harder sell than
arriving first into one that doesn't yet.

| Phase | Window | Exit gate (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 · Clean house | now → ~2026-10-05 (compressed from 10-12) | Name cleared, branding/legal residue gone, security reporting live, **first tagged preview** (2026-09-30 — starts awesome-selfhosted's 4-month clock) |
| 1 · Quiet preview | ~10-05 → 11-01 | Design-partner migrations, bundle connector, a scoped security review, docs + demo + compose live, NLnet proposal filed (**2026-11-03**) |
| **Checkpoint** | **2026-10-01** | Go/no-go read on every hard gate (doc 04). Anything not genuinely on track gets **descoped**, not the date pushed — doc 04 has the descope order |
| 2 · Launch | **Tue 2026-11-10 — the plan, not a maybe** | `v1.0.0`, Show HN, then staged channels |
| 3 · Consolidate | launch → 2027-03 (compressed) | awesome-selfhosted (eligible ~2027-01-30), app stores, support subscriptions |
| 4 · Expand | 2027-H1+ | Confluence DC campaign, hosting partner, EU integrator partner, LTS line, Confluence importer beta |

**2027-01-12 is retired as a comfortable fallback.** Keep it only as a name for "what a missed window
looks like": by then upstream's own "3.1" milestone (already 4/5 closed) says it will very plausibly
have a materially stronger public 3.0, and FOSDEM's amplification (Jan 30–31) would be arriving after
that news instead of ahead of it. If 2026-11-10 turns out to be genuinely unreachable even after
descoping, that is a strategic emergency to surface immediately — not a scheduling inconvenience to
absorb by sliding two months.

Do not launch in December regardless. Do not cut the security review to zero even under deadline
pressure (doc 04's descope order keeps a minimum scoped version) — a credibility failure at launch is
worse than losing the window it was trying to protect.

## 5. The honest risk picture

The biggest threat to Cardinal is not Docmost or upstream. It is **credibility**: a one-person,
AI-built project that stores other systems' credentials, launching into the post-Huntarr climate, with
an internal process history that an adversarial reader can mine (see `ngpixel-field-notes.md`:
duplicate bug filings, chronic red-CI tickets, features closed and broken the same day, a formatter
gate that exits 0 on a finding). The counter is not spin — it is doing the unglamorous trust work *in
public before anyone asks*: an external security review, published advisories, green CI that stays
green, a truthful maturity statement, and disclosure you volunteered rather than had extracted.

The second biggest threat is **time**: public users bring issues, questions and expectations that can
crowd out the reason Cardinal exists. If you do go public, Docmost is the lesson on what wins: it won its
category with a thinner feature set than Cardinal's, by shipping in public on a cadence and being
findable. Take on only as much of that as you enjoy. The premise above wins any conflict with the
rest of this set.
